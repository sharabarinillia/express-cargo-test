Four rules that catch shippers out.

30%
Maximum state of charge for lithium-ion batteries shipped on their own (UN3480), and since 1 January 2026 also for batteries packed with equipment.
0 kg
Lithium batteries shipped on their own (UN3480, UN3090) are forbidden on passenger aircraft: they fly on cargo aircraft only.
5 kg
Maximum net weight of lithium batteries per package on a passenger aircraft, when they are packed with or inside equipment.
38.3
Every cell and battery type must pass the UN 38.3 tests, and the manufacturer makes the test summary available.

Source: IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, 67th edition (2026), and the IATA Battery Guidance Document 2026.

How we prepare a lithium shipment.

5 steps · one coordinator

CHECK TEST PACK DOCS BOOK
  1. 01 / 05 · CHECK

    Classification

    We confirm the UN number, packing instruction and section from your battery data: chemistry, watt-hours or lithium content, and how the batteries travel.

    • Lithium-ion or lithium metal
    • Watt-hours or lithium content per cell and battery
    • On their own, packed with or inside equipment
  2. 02 / 05 · TEST

    Test summary & charge

    Every cell and battery type must have passed the UN 38.3 tests. We check the test summary and, for lithium-ion, the state of charge wherever the rules set a limit.

    • UN 38.3 test summary from the manufacturer
    • State of charge at or below 30% where required
    • No damaged or defective batteries
  3. 03 / 05 · PACK

    Packing & marks

    We arrange UN-specification packaging where it is required, and can pack and prepare the shipment ourselves, with the right marks and labels.

    • UN-specification packaging arranged
    • Terminals protected against short circuit
    • Class 9 label, battery mark and Cargo Aircraft Only label as required
  4. 04 / 05 · DOCS

    Documents

    The Shipper’s Declaration for Dangerous Goods, or the Section II statement on the air waybill, plus the customs documents.

    • Shipper’s Declaration for Section I shipments
    • Section II statement on the air waybill
    • Export and import customs documents
  5. 05 / 05 · BOOK

    Booking & delivery

    Booked with a carrier that accepts the batteries, on a cargo aircraft when the rules require it, or by road or sea when that suits better. Delivered door to door.

    • Carrier acceptance confirmed before booking
    • Cargo aircraft when the batteries require it
    • One coordinator from pickup to delivery

What we need from you.

Send what you have. If something is missing, we tell you exactly what, and why.

  1. 01Battery type: lithium-ion or lithium metal
  2. 02Watt-hours (lithium-ion) or lithium content in grams (lithium metal), per cell and per battery
  3. 03How they travel: on their own, packed with equipment or built in
  4. 04Number of batteries and net weight per package
  5. 05The UN 38.3 test summary from the manufacturer
  6. 06State of charge (lithium-ion), and confirmation that the batteries are not damaged or defective

Not accepted

Damaged or defective batteries.

We don’t accept damaged or defective lithium batteries, by any mode, even where a special approval might be possible. By air, batteries that are damaged or recalled for safety reasons are forbidden anyway (IATA Special Provision A154).

Medical equipment & healthcare

Lithium inside medical equipment.

Monitors, pumps, portable scanners: much of today’s medical equipment runs on lithium-ion, often with a spare pack in the box. That is where our experience lies: medical equipment, healthcare, pharmaceuticals and other specialised cargo.

  • Equipment with batteries inside (UN3481, PI 967)
  • Spare packs in the same box (PI 966, at 30% charge or less since 2026)
  • Temperature data loggers travelling with pharma
  • The complete process: air freight, road transport, customs clearance, documentation, special handling and time-critical delivery

By air, road and sea.

Subject to the applicable regulations, carrier acceptance and the classification of the batteries.

  • Air freight IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations. Cargo aircraft only for batteries on their own; passenger or cargo aircraft for batteries with or inside equipment.
  • Road transport ADR, the European agreement on dangerous goods by road, across Europe and beyond.
  • Sea freight The IMDG Code for dangerous goods at sea, for larger volumes and long distances.
